# economic-benefits-vs-political-loyalty
# Benefit or Loyalty: An empirical research from 2021 referendum in Taiwan

## Overview

This project examines whether citizens prioritize economic self-interest or partisan loyalty when forming policy preferences in a highly polarized political environment.

Previous research suggests that political parties play an important role in shaping public opinion and policy attitudes. However, it remains unclear whether citizens support policies that benefit them economically or whether they follow the positions of the political parties with which they identify.

Taiwan provides an ideal case for studying this question. Unlike many Western democracies, political competition in Taiwan is structured primarily around national identity rather than traditional left-right economic ideology. As a result, party positions on economic and trade issues do not necessarily align with voters' material interests.

Using township-level referendum results from Taiwan's 2021 National Referendum, this project evaluates whether support for policy proposals is better explained by local economic interests or partisan alignment.

---

## Research Question

**Do economic interests or partisan loyalty play a larger role in shaping policy preferences in a polarized political environment?**

Specifically, this project investigates whether support for a ban on U.S. pork imports containing ractopamine was driven by:

1. Economic exposure to the pork industry.
2. Political alignment with Taiwan's major political parties.

---

## Case Selection: Taiwan's 2021 National Referendum

The 2021 referendum provides a useful setting for studying policy preferences because:

* Referendum results capture revealed policy preferences from the entire electorate rather than a survey sample.
* Township-level referendum and demographic data are publicly available.
* Taiwan's major political parties are divided primarily by national identity rather than economic ideology.
* Aggregate electoral data avoids common survey issues such as self-reporting bias and nonresponse bias.

### Referendum Proposals

#### Proposal 17: Fourth Nuclear Power Plant

Should the Fourth Nuclear Power Plant resume commercial operation?

**Issue Area:** Energy policy, nuclear safety, electricity supply

#### Proposal 18: Ban on Ractopamine Pork Imports

Should Taiwan ban imports of pork products from pigs treated with ractopamine?

**Issue Area:** Food safety, trade policy, international relations

#### Proposal 19: Referendums and Elections Held on the Same Day

Should referendums be held concurrently with national elections?

**Issue Area:** Electoral institutions and political participation

#### Proposal 20: Relocation of the Third LNG Receiving Terminal

Should the planned LNG terminal be relocated away from the Guantang coastal ecosystem?

**Issue Area:** Environmental protection and energy policy

## Data

### Dependent Variables

* Township-level agreement rates for Proposals 17–20 in the 2021 National Referendum.

### Key Independent Variables

#### Economic Interest

* Number of pig farms by township.
* Number of cattle farms by township (placebo test).

#### Partisan Alignment

* Vote share of the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) in the 2020 presidential election.
* Vote share of the Kuomintang (KMT) in the 2020 presidential election.

### Control Variables

* Demographic and socioeconomic characteristics at the township level.

---

## Hypotheses

### H1: Economic Self-Interest

Townships with more pig farms will exhibit higher support for Proposal 18 (ban on pork imports), but not necessarily for the other referendum proposals.

**Expectation:** The import policy directly affects the economic interests of pork producers.

### H2: Placebo Test

The number of cattle farms will not significantly affect support for Proposal 18 or the other referendum proposals.

**Expectation:** The cattle industry is not directly affected by pork import policy.

### H3: Supporters of the Ruling Party Follow Party Cues

Higher DPP vote share will be associated with lower agreement rates across the four referendum proposals.

**Expectation:** Voters align with the position of the ruling party.

### H4: Supporters of the Opposition Follow Party Cues

Higher KMT vote share will be associated with higher agreement rates across the four referendum proposals.

**Expectation:** Voters align with the position of the main opposition party.

---

## Methods

* Township-level statistical analysis
* Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression
* Geographic and demographic data integration
* Electoral data analysis

---

## Key Question

When economic interests and partisan signals point in different directions, which one matters more?

This project uses Taiwan's 2021 referendum to explore how citizens make policy decisions in a polarized democracy and whether partisan loyalty can outweigh material self-interest.
